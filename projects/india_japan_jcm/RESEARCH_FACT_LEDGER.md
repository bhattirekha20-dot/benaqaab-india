# Research Fact Ledger — India–Japan JCM Short

**Research date:** 2026-10-06
**Format:** YouTube Short, vertical 1080×1920, maximum 2:00
**Working title:** India–Japan Carbon Credit Mechanism: Climate Finance Ya Carbon Accounting?

## Confirmed primary facts

1. India and Japan signed a Memorandum of Cooperation on the Joint Crediting Mechanism under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement in August 2025. The official PIB release says the mechanism is intended to encourage investment, technology transfer and capacity building for low-carbon projects, and enable international trading of generated carbon credits under Article 6.2. Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2161892
2. The official Rules of Implementation PDF states that the JCM is a cooperative approach in which Japan and Japanese public/private entities cooperate with India and Indian public/private entities to implement mitigation activities in India. Source: https://moef.gov.in/storage/tender/1784699228.pdf
3. Under the Rules of Implementation, JCM credits are calculated as the difference between reference emissions and project emissions. The reference emissions must be below business-as-usual emissions with a conservative buffer so the activity contributes to India’s NDC. Source: same MoEF PDF.
4. Credit sharing is decided by the Joint Committee with prior approval from both governments, based on technical and financial evaluation. Source: same MoEF PDF.
5. Each government maintains a registry. The Rules require status checks and corresponding adjustments to avoid double counting. Source: same MoEF PDF.
6. Third-party entities validate proposed projects and verify achieved greenhouse-gas emission reductions or removals. Source: same MoEF PDF.
7. A JCM project selects a crediting period of no longer than ten years, including renewal if any, subject to approval. Source: same MoEF PDF.
8. Some JCM credits may be used as internationally transferred mitigation outcomes for Japan’s NDC or another authorised international mitigation purpose, but authorisation and accounting rules apply. Source: same MoEF PDF.
9. India’s domestic carbon market framework assigns the Bureau of Energy Efficiency responsibilities around carbon credit certificates, verification agencies, registries and trading procedures. This is separate from, but relevant to, understanding the domestic context. Source: https://beeindia.gov.in/show_content.php?lang=1&level=1&ls_id=189&lid=294

## What the Short must not claim

- That Japan is simply buying permission to pollute.
- That every Indian carbon reduction automatically becomes a Japanese credit.
- That all JCM credits are already being traded at scale.
- That the mechanism guarantees emissions reductions without project-level verification.
- That the JCM is identical to India’s domestic Carbon Credit Trading Scheme.
- A specific project list, credit price, credit quantity or project allocation unless a current official document confirms it.

## Editorial angle

The Short explains the mechanism in plain language and asks the accountability question: can Japanese finance and technology accelerate real Indian decarbonisation while India retains credible accounting and avoids double counting?

## Confirmed / analysis / uncertainty labels

- Confirmed: MoC, Rules of Implementation, Joint Committee, reference-versus-project emissions, verification, registries, corresponding adjustments and ten-year maximum crediting period.
- Analysis: the mechanism could make clean projects easier to finance, but environmental integrity depends on baselines, monitoring, verification and credit allocation.
- Uncertainty: the available primary documents do not establish a large live portfolio or a current market price. Do not invent either.
