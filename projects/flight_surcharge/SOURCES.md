# SOURCES.md — फ्लाइट क्यों महँगी (IndiGo fuel surcharge film)

**Topic:** IndiGo re-introduced a **fuel surcharge from 00:01 hrs on 6 October 2026** — roughly
**₹375–₹1,300 on domestic** routes and **₹1,000–₹10,000 on international** — after ATF prices jumped
more than 14 % month-on-month to a decade high, driven by the West Asia conflict. Reported 5 Oct 2026.

**Why this topic:** it went live this morning, it hits every flyer's wallet in the festive season,
the numbers are precise (a five-tier domestic table + a five-step ATF price ladder), and it has a real
honesty layer (this is the second surcharge round since February; crude was cooling again by 5 Oct;
the airline claims it is recovering less than its actual cost rise; the other carriers had not matched
the move yet).

- IndiGo controls **nearly two-thirds of India's domestic air traffic**.

## N6 — The March 2026 surcharge round (precedent)

  them **around July** as prices stabilised.
- ET, 14 Mar 2026: Akasa announced a surcharge of **₹199–₹1,300** per sector from 15 March, following

## N7 — Who else has announced (as of 5 Oct 2026)
- Only **IndiGo** had announced this round; Air India declined comment, no announcement from
  SpiceJet/Akasa reported for this round. Precedent (March) says the others eventually matched.

## Film rules for this story
1. The surcharge is **IndiGo's** — never framed as an industry-wide or government move.
2. "सिर्फ़ नई बुकिंग पर लागू" is stated in the film (it does not raise existing tickets).
3. The March→July precedent and the cooling crude (Brent ≈ $101 on 5 Oct) are inside the film, so
   nobody reads the surcharge as permanent.
4. Distances on screen are marked approximate; every rupee figure carries its tier.
5. No photographs, no airline logos — the plane, fuel gauge, ladder and map are drawn in code.
6. Official CapCut transition names only, reconstructed natively in `viz/motion_library.js`.
