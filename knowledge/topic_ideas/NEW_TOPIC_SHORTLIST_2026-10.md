## 1. Why the Bay of Bengal gets ~4× more cyclones than the Arabian Sea
**Hook (Hinglish):** "Duniya ke 10% cyclone India me aate hain — par 4 me se 3 Bengal ki taraf. Kyun?"

**Verified facts**
- India is exposed to nearly **10 % of the world's tropical cyclones**; **5–6 cyclones form every year**,
  2–3 of which can become severe. Coastline **8,041 km**.
- Bay of Bengal : Arabian Sea ratio ≈ **4 : 1**.
- Last century: of **1,019 cyclonic disturbances** over the subcontinent, **890 were on the east coast** —
  **260 of those made landfall over Odisha alone**.
- Reasons that are actually physical: Bay SST **28–30 °C**; the bay is a **three-sided basin** that traps
  heat and moisture; huge river discharge (Ganga, Brahmaputra) creates a **low-salinity surface layer**
  that keeps warm water on top; lower vertical wind shear. The Arabian Sea has **upwelling** (cool water
  brought up) and higher shear.
- **The counter-trend to show honestly:** the Arabian Sea is now **warming faster**, and recent years have
  seen unusually intense Arabian Sea cyclones. The "4:1 forever" story is the old normal, not the whole
  story.

**Sources:** India Water Portal FAQ on tropical cyclones in India (IMD-based) ·
`indiawaterportal.org/climate-change/disasters/faq-on-tropical-cyclones-in-india-2` ·
Lukmaan IAS brief on uncommon Arabian Sea cyclones.

**What we animate:** a true-to-scale map of India; warm-layer cross-section with the fresh-water lid;
two cyclone tracks drawn to the same scale; a 4:1 counter filling; a temperature gauge per sea.
**Runtime:** ~50–60 s. **Risk:** don't imply we can predict cyclones. Careful tone — this kills people.

## 2. UPI: how India moves ₹314 lakh crore on almost nothing
**Hook (Hinglish):** "Ek second me 3,729 payments. Uptime 99.99%. Aur duniya ke aadhe real-time
payments India me hote hain — par ye chalta kaise hai?"

**Verified facts**
- **FY 2025-26: 24,162 crore transactions, ₹314 lakh crore**, per NPCI / Ministry of Finance.
- **May 2026: 23.2 billion transactions worth ₹29.9 trillion** — the highest month on record;
  daily average **737.79 million transactions** (~₹84,423 crore/day).
- Growth arc: **April 2016 launch — 21 banks, 373 transactions in the first month** → **703 banks** live.
- **3,729 transactions per second**, **99.99 % uptime**, **491 million users** (Sept 2025 reporting).
- **≈46–49 % of the world's real-time payment volume** is India's (IMF, June 2025).
- Average transaction value ≈ **₹1,300** — and **86 % of merchant payments are below ₹500**.
- NPCI runs UPI + IMPS + NACH on a cost base reported around **₹500 crore**.

**Sources:** ANI report on NPCI May-2026 data · Mint (Dec 2025 NPCI data) · India Today (Sept 2025) ·
NPCI product statistics (open this at production).

**What we animate:** one payment travelling — payer app → bank → NPCI switch → beneficiary bank → done —
with a live-magnitude timer; a 3,729-per-second counter; the 2016→2026 growth curve on one linear axis
(373 → 24,162 crore is a scale honesty problem worth solving on screen, not hiding).
**Runtime:** ~45–55 s. **Risk:** none factual, but avoid implying "no failures ever" — uptime ≠ zero failures.

## 3. Why Delhi's air gets trapped every winter (the invisible lid)
**Hook (Hinglish):** "Delhi ki hawa gandi hoti hai — par winter me wo *jaati kyun nahi*? Jawab zameen se
500 metre upar hai."

**Verified facts**
- Winter **temperature inversion**: warmer air sits above colder surface air like a lid; vertical mixing
  stops. Documented case: at ~**500 m the air was ~25 °C while the surface was ~18 °C** — the opposite of
  normal.
- **Mixing height collapses**: from about **1–2 km in summer** to **200–500 m in winter** (CPCB-cited
  figures range ~1,500 m → under 300 m). Same emissions, far smaller volume of air.
- Delhi sits in a **bowl-shaped basin** between the Himalayas and the Aravallis, which restricts horizontal
  flushing (IITM 2021).
- Winter **calm winds** (often below ~2–5 km/h) remove dispersion; dry air removes rain washout; fog
  droplets help secondary aerosol form.
- Scale for the counter: WHO PM2.5 guideline **5 µg/m³**; Delhi winter peaks around **300–450 µg/m³**.

**Sources:** The Conversation (Jan 2026) · Hindustan Times (Nov 2023, IMD/Skymet quotes) ·
IITM 2021 basin study · CPCB annual air quality report.

**What we animate:** a cross-section of Delhi's atmosphere with a movable "lid"; particles released at
street level bouncing under it; a mixing-height gauge that collapses from summer to winter; a wind-speed
dial. **Runtime:** ~50–60 s. **Risk:** publishable numbers vary by source — show the range, attribute each.

## 4. The monsoon's first rain: why Kerala, why 1 June
**Hook (Hinglish):** "1 June — IMD 'monsoon aa gaya' kehta hai. Par wo declare kaise karte hain?
Aur Somali jet kya cheez hai?"

**Verified facts (IMD's own criteria)**
- Onset is declared when **at least 60 % of 14 designated southern stations record ≥2.5 mm rain for two
  consecutive days**; westerlies at **925 hPa of 15–20 knots**, with the depth of westerlies maintained up
  to **600 hPa**; **OLR below 200 W/m²** (deep convection).
- Mechanism: the **ITCZ** migrates north with the sun and reaches ~**8 °N near Kerala around 1 June**;
  southeast trades cross the equator, are deflected right by the Coriolis force and become the
  **southwest monsoon**; the **Somali jet** — a low-level cross-equatorial jet — strengthens the onset.
- Progression: Kerala ~1 June → Delhi ~1 July → **whole country by ~15 July**; withdrawal begins in NW
  India around **1 September**. India gets **~75–90 % of its annual rain in June–September**;
  long-period average **87 cm** (1971–2020).
- Contrast to draw: **Mawsynram ~11,871 mm/year vs Jaisalmer ~160 mm/year**.

**Sources:** IMD onset criteria (Shankari IAS current-affairs summary, May 2025) · ClearIAS mechanism ·
IMD LPA figures.

**What we animate:** a globe strip with the ITCZ belt moving north; Somali jet arrow off Madagascar;
evaporation over the Arabian Sea; orographic lift over the Western Ghats; Mawsynram/Jaisalmer on a shared
rainfall scale. **Runtime:** ~55–65 s. **Risk:** the "burst" is a media moment — show the measurement,
not drama.

## 5. Lakshadweep's corals: what the numbers actually say
**Hook (Hinglish):** "Ek hi reef ke liye teen studies — 82 %, 73 %, 31 %. Kaun sa number sach hai?
Teeno — aur yahi baat sabse interesting hai."

**Verified facts (this topic's whole point is the disagreement)**
- **1998 (El Niño event):** bleached coral was **82 % of coral cover in Lakshadweep lagoon reefs** and
  **89 % in the Gulf of Mannar**; in the Gulf of Kutch only **11 %**, with no apparent bleaching-related
  mortality.
- **2010 (Agatti):** **73 % of corals bleached**; apparent mortality in sea anemones **87 %**, giant clams
  **83 %**; maximum recorded SST up to **34 °C**.
- **2016:** **82 % of reefs bleached with 26 % mortality** at degree heating weeks (DHW) **6.17**;
  a separate report puts mortality at **31 %**.
- Thermal context: Lakshadweep bleaching threshold ≈ **30.4 °C** (warmest month May, mean 29.90 ± 0.49 °C);
  observed warming ≈ **0.2 °C per decade** (1985–2010 satellite data).
- Why the numbers differ: different reefs, depths, survey dates and metrics — bleaching % ≠ mortality %.
  Show that on screen.

**Sources:** bioRxiv systematic review of Indian reef bleaching (2024) · Vinoth et al. 2010/2011 Agatti
survey · 1998 Indian reef regions study · ScienceDirect Lakshadweep environmental-variables paper.

**What we animate:** a coral polyp with its zooxanthellae; a temperature curve crossing the 30.4 °C
threshold; a time bar where the three studies appear against the same axis; recovery vs death branching.
**Runtime:** ~55–65 s. **Risk:** don't accuse anyone — the honest point is that measurement context changes
the number.

## 6. Why your flight home is 2 hours longer than the flight there
**Hook (Hinglish):** "Delhi se New York ki flight, aur New York se Delhi — distance same, time nahi.
Aur ye Earth ke ghoomne ki wajah se nahi hai. Asli wajah upar 10 km pe hai."

**Verified facts**
- The jet stream is a west-to-east river of air at cruise altitude; flying east you ride it, flying west
  you fight it. Eastbound flights are typically **30–90 minutes faster**; London–New York can differ by
  up to **~2 hours**.
- Maths we can draw: **500 mph airspeed + 120 mph tailwind = 620 mph ground speed**;
  **500 − 120 = 380 mph** against it. Same aircraft, same airspeed.
- Jet stream speeds commonly **80–140 mph**, and **200+ mph in winter**, because the polar-to-tropical
  temperature contrast is strongest then.
- **The myth to kill:** Earth's rotation does not *directly* make eastbound flights faster — the atmosphere
  rotates with the planet. It is the Coriolis-driven jet stream that does. (One recorded case: a flight
  hitting a supercharged jet stream reached a **ground speed of 825 mph** while remaining subsonic.)
- Airlines plan around it: eastbound routed through the core, westbound detoured (even flying farther) to
  dodge the strongest headwind.

**Sources:** ScienceInsights jet-stream explainer (Mar 2026) · travel/aviation explainers ·
Business Insider Coriolis explainer. **Before production:** verify the Delhi–US route timings from an
official airline schedule.

**What we animate:** a globe with the jet stream as a ribbon; two aircraft with airspeed and ground-speed
vectors; the same great-circle path drawn twice; a headwind/tailwind bar that flips.
**Runtime:** ~45–55 s. **Risk:** the "Earth's rotation" claim is the whole hook — phrase it precisely.

## 7. Chandrayaan-3 measured the Moon's south pole — and the number surprised scientists
**Hook (Hinglish):** "Surface pe ~50 °C. Sirf 8 cm neeche −10 °C. Ye ISRO ke ChaSTE ne naapa — lunar
south pole ka pehla temperature profile."

**Verified facts**
- **ChaSTE (Chandra's Surface Thermophysical Experiment)**, developed by VSSC's Space Physics Laboratory
  with PRL Ahmedabad, is a **10-sensor probe driven up to 10 cm** into the lunar soil.
- First profile of the lunar south pole (released 27 Aug 2023): at the surface **40–50 °C** (up to ~50–60 °C
  just above it), dropping to about **−10 °C at 8 cm depth** — a ~50–60 °C fall in a few centimetres.
- Landing site: between the craters **Manzinus C and Simpelius N**.
- Context to draw on the same scale: permanently shadowed crater floors near the pole can be as cold as
  about **40 K above absolute zero (≈ −233 °C)**.
- The −10 °C at depth was **warmer than expected** — regolith is a poor conductor, so the top layer cooks
  and insulates.

**Sources:** The Hindu and The Hindu BusinessLine coverage of ISRO's released graph (Aug 2023) ·
ISRO/PRL instrument description. **Before production:** open ISRO's own release/chart.

**What we animate:** the lander with the probe driving into regolith, the 10 sensors lighting up, a
temperature-vs-depth curve drawn live, and a side-by-side scale with a shadowed crater floor.
**Runtime:** ~45–55 s. **Risk:** don't merge the probe's numbers with other missions' surface readings.
